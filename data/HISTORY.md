# Lafayette history by era (time-travel mode)
Compiled 2026-10-19 (PT). Companion file: `history_pois.geojson` with 26 features. 21 have coordinates; 5 have `geometry: null` because the site couldn't be located. Unknown years are `null`. Every fact has a source, and **GAP** marks what is still missing.

## Era framing corrections
- **Hwy 24 came earlier than the 1970s.** Through Lafayette the freeway was first built c.1957–59: Acalanes Rd to I-680 c.1957, Wilder Rd to Acalanes Rd c.1959. Those segments were rebuilt or reopened Dec 1969 and Nov 1970 [https://www.aaroads.com/guides/ca-024]. Construction began 1954–56 [https://eastbayhillsproject.org/gallery/how-eminent-domain-saved-lamorinda-part-6-breaking-the-bottleneck-and-imminent-departure-at-the-bypass/]. Before the freeway, Hwy 24 (designated 1932) ran along Mt Diablo Blvd [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Route_24_(California)].
- **Tunnels:** the Kennedy Tunnel opened in 1903, its ceiling was raised in 1915 for autos, and the twin Broadway Low Level bores opened Dec 5, 1937. They were renamed Caldecott in 1960. The third bore opened in Oct 1964 and the fourth in 2013 [https://lafayettehistory.org/mtdiabloblvd/ ; https://www.aaroads.com/guides/ca-024].
- **Confirmed dates:** the Reservoir opened to the public June 13, 1966; BART opened May 21, 1973; the city incorporated July 1968. So the 1950s are the freeway-arrival decade, and the 1960s–70s bring recreation, the city and BART.
- **Sacramento Northern** (1913–41 passenger, freight to 1957) belongs in the orchard era. It was gone before the freeway and BART.
- **Source conflicts:**
  - Naming year: 1857 per the City and the LHS Village Life page, 1855 per the LHS Tour page.
  - Trail opening: 1974 per lmhistorytrail.org, dedicated July 4, 1976 per EBRPD.

## Era 1: c.1847–1880s, settlement and rancho
**Buildings and roads**
- **Rancho Acalanes (Elam Brown purchase)** (1847–): 3,329-acre Mexican land grant (granted 1834 to Candelario Valencia) bought by Elam Brown for $900 in 1847. It covers almost all of present-day Lafayette. Point is the city centroid, not a building. [https://www.lovelafayette.org/why-lafayette/about-lafayette/history-of-lafayette ; https://lafayettehistory.org/local-sites/tour/]
- **Elam Brown's first home (Happy Valley area)** (1848–): Brown moved into the Happy Valley area Feb 7, 1848. His first permanent home was a pre-cut redwood frame house about two miles north of Lafayette. Exact site unknown. [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/yankee-settlers/]
- **Brown's grist mill** (1853–): Grist mill on Lafayette Creek at Golden Gate Way near First St. It pulled the village centre toward Mt Diablo Blvd/Moraga Rd in the early 1860s. [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/early-days/]
- **Milo Hough's Hotel** (1853–): First of three hotels near the Plaza. Later sold to Benjamin Shreve. [https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/]
- **Plaza (Elam Brown's gift)** (1864–): 100x150 ft triangle deeded to the town Nov 19, 1864. By the early 1860s it was ringed by the mill, a store, a school, a blacksmith and a hotel. [https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/ ; https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-town/]
- **Pioneer Store (Shreve's store & post office)** (1862–): Benjamin Shreve's store, post office and insurance agency, opened at this site in the early 1860s. Bought by Robert Elam McNeil in 1902. Still standing, remodeled with a false front. [https://lafayettehistory.org/local-sites/tour/ ; https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/village-life/]
- **Lafayette Cemetery** (1874–): 4.5-acre hillside bought for $100 by the Cemetery Corporation, chaired by Elam Brown, in 1874. [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/village-life/]
- The road from the Moraga/Canyon redwoods to Martinez came over Jonas Hill into today's Golden Gate Way near Second St, where the early school and church stood. By 1855 the redwood traffic supported a blacksmith and taverns [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/early-days/].
- Pony Express horse change at Mt Diablo/Moraga, early 1860s [https://www.lovelafayette.org/why-lafayette/about-lafayette/history-of-lafayette].

**Land and vegetation:** Brown first ran only cattle, then planted wheat in spring 1848, and the county became the state's top wheat area [https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/]. Nathaniel Jones planted California's first black locusts ("Locust Farm") [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/yankee-settlers/]. A great flood hit in Jan 1862 [https://lafayettehistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Newsletter-April-2023.pdf].

**Vehicles:** wagons and teams hauling redwood lumber, mule trains [https://lmhistorytrail.org/], Pony Express horses. **GAP:** vehicle types beyond that.

**People:** Elam Brown, Margaret Allen Brown, Nathaniel Jones, Benjamin Shreve, Milo Hough, Candelario Valencia, William Leidesdorff, and the Thomson, Bradley, Hough, Lamp and Allen families (sources in LAFAYETTE_RESEARCH.md §4 and https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/).

## Era 2: c.1890s–1930s, orchards and the early town
**Buildings and roads**
- **Hotel corner (Hastings / Lamp hotels)** (None–1925): Mt Diablo Blvd & Moraga Rd. Norman Hastings' hotel burned in 1892, and Phillip Lamp's hotel, also on this corner, burned in 1925. No hotels were built here after that. [https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/]
- **Wayside Inn** (1894–): Two-story hotel and tavern built by Edward J. Brady in 1894 for cattle drovers. Bought by Pat Medau in 1921 and used as an ice cream parlor from 1925. [https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/ ; https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-town/]
- **Post office (Carrie Van Meter)** (1904–1927): Post office on Mt Diablo Blvd. Postmistress Carrie Van Meter (1904-27) also lent its 60 books, making her the first librarian. Exact site unknown. [https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/]
- **Town Hall** (1914–): Built 1914 by volunteers and the Lafayette Improvement Association. Dances here ran late enough that a special 3 AM train took partygoers home. [https://www.townhalltheatre.com/mission-and-history ; https://lafayettehistory.org/tunnelsandroads/]
- **West Lafayette station (Sacramento Northern)** (1913–1941): Electric interurban station at the end of School St, near Paradise Ct. Service through Lafayette began in 1913, passenger service ended in 1941 and freight in 1957. [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-changing-pace/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Northern_Railway]
- **Cornfield (future La Fiesta Shopping Center)** (None–1915): Before 1915 a cornfield grew below Mt Diablo Blvd along Moraga Rd. [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-town/]
- **Lafayette United Methodist Church (1893 school building)** (1927–): The congregation moved in 1927 into the former 1893 third school building. [https://thelumc.org/our-history]
- **Lafayette Reservoir dam & outlet tower** (1927–1933): George Pollock Co. contract, July 1927. A slide in Sept 1928 forced a lower, wider redesign, and the dam was completed in 1933. [https://lafayettehistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Newsletter-April-2023.pdf]
- **Starks' Lafayette Meat Market** (1920–): Moved in 1920 to the SE corner of Mt Diablo Blvd & Hough Ave. Rebuilt 1935 as a 'uniquely-shaped' store and remodeled in 1949. [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-town/]
- **Clarence Brown's Hardware** (None–): 1930s 'Main Street' style store on the NE corner of Mt Diablo Blvd & Oak Hill Rd. [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-town/]
- **Garrett Building** (1937–): Turreted brick building by realtor Col. N. M. Garrett, 1937. [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-town/]
- In 1910 the Plaza was ringed by the Geils Building, Pioneer Store, Lafayette Meat Market, Lafayette Hotel and Peter Thomson's blacksmith shop [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-town/].
- Tunnel Road (today's Mt Diablo Blvd) was paved from the tunnel to Walnut Creek in 1916 but was still dirt through town in 1926. In the 1920s cattle were driven down it through the tunnel to Emeryville. By 1937 it had lane lines but no stop lights or signs [https://lafayettehistory.org/mtdiabloblvd/].
- The Sacramento Northern ran parallel to modern Beechwood Dr. Olympic Blvd now covers the old right-of-way at Reliez Station Rd [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-changing-pace/].

**Land and vegetation:** orchards of pears, prunes, plums and walnuts. The Malley "Peardale" ranch had pears, peaches, walnuts and vineyards [https://lafayettehistory.org/oral-history-marianne-millette-malley-march-5th-2014/ ; https://lafayettehistory.org/oral-history-charles-reynolds-march-20th-1979/]. Burton Valley orchards bordered the tracks [https://eastbayhillsproject.org/gallery/orchards-of-lafayettes-burton-valley-moraga-border-to-the-station/]. Oil-stock schemes on the Flood Ranch (1901, 1921) never produced oil [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-town/].

**Vehicles:** wagons in the old tunnel, which was so narrow that hay wagons drove down the middle [https://lafayettehistory.org/town-history/pictorial-history/the-changing-pace/]. Single-car electric interurbans and observation cars [https://lafayettehistory.org/tunnelsandroads/]. **"Old Betsy"**, a c.1920 Model T fire truck, is now on display in the library [https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/].

**People:** Robert Elam McNeil, Carrie Van Meter, Pat and Lizzie Medau, Edward J. Brady, Col. N. M. Garrett, Clarence and Freda Brown, the Starks, M.H. Stanley, the Lafayette Improvement Club, and George Pollock Co. and Leo J. Coleman at the dam.

## Era 3: c.1940s–1950s, boulevard before and during the freeway
**Buildings and roads**
- **Acalanes High School** (1940–): Opened Sept 10, 1940 with 406 students. [https://lafayettehistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Newsletter-January-2024.pdf]
- **Park Theater** (1941–2005): Opened Aug 21, 1941. Streamline Moderne with a neon marquee. Closed 2005, reopening targeted for early 2027. [https://parktheatertrust.org/history/]
- **Veterans Memorial Building (WPA, demolished)** (1939–): WPA building of 1938-39 at Mt Diablo Blvd & First St. Replaced in 2005, and its site became the library. [https://livingnewdeal.org/sites/old-veterans-memorial-building-lafayette-ca/]
- **Hwy 24 Lafayette bypass** (1957–): The freeway through Lafayette was originally built c.1957-59 (Wilder Rd to Acalanes Rd c.1959, Acalanes Rd to I-680 c.1957) and rebuilt or reopened 1969-70. Before that, Hwy 24 ran along Mt Diablo Blvd. [https://www.aaroads.com/guides/ca-024 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Route_24_(California)]
- In the 1940s Plaza Park was surrounded by King's Restaurant, the Park Theater, the Wayside Inn, the Pioneer Store and a gas station [https://lafayettehistory.org/earlydowntown/].
- The housing boom began in the early 1950s, replacing orchards [https://eastbayhillsproject.org/gallery/orchards-of-lafayettes-burton-valley-moraga-border-to-the-station/].

**Vehicles:** 1940s–50s autos, plus the Sacramento Northern freight until Mar 1, 1957 [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Northern_Railway]. **GAP:** sourced photos of specific models.

**People:** Elwood P. Laws and William B. David (Park Theater), and the Lafayette-Orinda Presbyterian congregation that met in the theater from 1954.

## Era 4: c.1960s–1970s, freeway, reservoir, city and BART
- **Lafayette Reservoir Recreation Area opens** (1966–): EBMUD opened it to the public June 13, 1966. [https://www.ebmud.com/customers/pipeline/2016/05/50-years-reservoir-fun]
- **Lafayette BART station** (1973–): Opened May 21, 1973 in the Hwy 24 median. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_station_(BART)]
- **Lafayette-Moraga Trail** (1974–): Rail-trail on the former Sacramento Northern right-of-way. Sources disagree on the date: opened 1974 per the LM History Trail, dedicated July 4, 1976 per EBRPD. [https://lmhistorytrail.org/ ; https://www.ebparks.org/about-us/whats-new/news/45-years-lafayette-moraga-regional-trail]
- **Caldecott third bore** (1964–): The third bore opened Oct 1964, after the 1937 twin bores. Outside Lafayette, to the west. [https://www.aaroads.com/guides/ca-024]
- 1960s Mt Diablo Blvd had four lanes with a centre median and overhead lights, and Safeway on the north side. In the 1970s downtown was "blanketed" with business signs until the sign ordinance [https://lafayettehistory.org/mtdiabloblvd/].
- The city incorporated in July 1968 [https://www.lovelafayette.org/home/showpublisheddocument/5367/636673441980530000].

**People:** Avon Wilson and Lynn Hiden (trail), and EBMUD.
